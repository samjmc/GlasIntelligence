# Scenario Comparison: Pharmacy First Payment Caps

> **Executive Summary** comparing two counterfactual policy scenarios for the NHS Pharmacy First service, simulated using multi-agent AI prediction (Glas Intelligence/OASIS).

| | Scenario 1: Caps Maintained | Scenario 2: Caps Removed |
|---|---|---|

## Head-to-Head Comparison

### Financial Viability

| Metric | Caps Maintained | Caps Removed |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-term pharmacy profitability** | Severe decline; 47% already unprofitable at EBITDA | Modest improvement (5-8% EBITDA uplift) |
| **Independent pharmacy risk** | Existential; unable to cover consultation costs | Reduced but persistent; volume inequality remains |
| **Chain pharmacy impact** | Question ROI of Pharmacy First | Benefit disproportionately from higher volumes |
| **Budget sustainability** | Controlled but at cost of service quality | 15-20% faster spend; fiscal cliff by Q3/Q4 |

### Workforce

|---|---|---|

| **Junior pharmacist retention** | Cannot support clinical career goals | Marginally better; structural issues remain |

---

## Key Insight

Neither scenario resolves the fundamental challenge: **delivering expanded clinical services through community pharmacy within a funding envelope that has not kept pace with demand or inflation.**

- **Caps Maintained** creates an immediate operational crisis -- pharmacies ration services, staff leave, patients lose access, and the Pharmacy First initiative risks public failure.

- **Caps Removed** provides breathing room but creates a **medium-term fiscal cliff**. Without additional CPCF funding, NHS England faces choosing between retrospective payment cuts (destroying trust), reintroducing caps (political reversal), or finding emergency budget (unlikely given Treasury constraints).

---

## Recommendation

The simulation evidence suggests that **removing caps is a necessary but insufficient reform**. The optimal policy path combines:

1. **Immediate cap removal** to restore pharmacist willingness to deliver Pharmacy First at scale
2. **CPCF funding uplift** to at least match inflation and new service demand (estimated £200-300m additional)
3. **Workforce investment ring-fencing** within CPCF to address the 75% staffing inadequacy finding
4. **Volume-based equity mechanisms** to prevent chain pharmacies from capturing disproportionate benefit
5. **Graduated budget management** (quarterly reviews rather than monthly caps) to balance fiscal control with operational flexibility
