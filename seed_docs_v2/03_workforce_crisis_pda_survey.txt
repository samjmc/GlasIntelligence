PDA WORKFORCE SURVEY JANUARY 2025
Source: Pharmacists' Defence Association (PDA) survey, January 2025

STAFFING ADEQUACY
75% of pharmacists do NOT believe their pharmacies are adequately staffed to safely deliver Pharmacy First. 93% to 94% reported NO increase in staffing despite new service demands. 49% already lacked sufficient staff for existing services BEFORE Pharmacy First launched on 31 January 2024.

MANDATED STANDARDS
95% of respondents support mandated minimum staffing levels. 97% agreed that mandated protected learning time should support service expansions.

PRESSURE AND PREPAREDNESS
73% reported pressure to meet consultation thresholds. 60% cited pressure from area managers specifically. Only 22% felt well-prepared for the service launch on 31 January 2024. Over two-thirds reported no protected learning time or financial compensation for training.

SUPPORT FOR REFORM
69% support continuing Pharmacy First but call for increased resourcing and removal of payment thresholds.

ENGAGEMENT WITH GOVERNMENT
The PDA shared these results directly with DHSC (Department of Health and Social Care) and NHS England officials. The PDA has warned of a workforce exodus and demands minimum staffing standards, seeking urgent talks with NHS England.

IMPLICATIONS
The survey suggests that Pharmacy First was rolled out without corresponding investment in workforce capacity. The combination of no staffing increase (93-94%), pressure to meet thresholds (73%), and lack of protected learning time creates conditions for burnout and patient safety concerns. Union representatives have linked these findings to an exodus of pharmacists from community pharmacy to GP practice or hospital roles where workload and pay are perceived as more sustainable.

SURVEY METHODOLOGY
The PDA survey was conducted in January 2025 and reflects the views of pharmacists working in community pharmacy settings across England. Results were shared with DHSC and NHS England to inform policy discussions. The 95% support for mandated minimum staffing suggests strong sector consensus for regulatory intervention, but no such standards have been introduced as of the 2025/26 settlement.
