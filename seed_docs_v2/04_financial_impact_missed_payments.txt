FINANCIAL IMPACT OF PHARMACY FIRST
Source: Pharmaceutical Journal, Chemist+Druggist, Frontier Economics, IQVIA Economic Analysis

MISSED THRESHOLD PAYMENTS
£71.69 million in total missed threshold payments from launch (January 2024) through May 2025. 6,551 pharmacies (62% of England's approximately 10,600 pharmacies) failed to meet the 30-consultation threshold in May 2025. The number of pharmacies failing to meet thresholds increased almost every time the threshold rose.

PROFITABILITY
Frontier Economics and IQVIA Economic Analysis found that 47% of pharmacy branches were not profitable at EBITDA level. Mean full economic cost to operate a pharmacy: £409,000 to £573,000. Sector total cost: £5.063 billion. Nearly 100% of pharmacies receive less funding than their full economic cost.

COST VS REIMBURSEMENT
Pharmaceutical Journal survey found the actual average cost of Pharmacy First consultations: £22 to £28, exceeding the £17 cap. The Pharmacy First budget was described as "shockingly underspent" in the first half of 2024/25.

JUNE 2025 PAYMENT SNAPSHOT
1,565 pharmacies (approximately 15% of the network) received the new £500 interim payment in June 2025. 4,051 pharmacies (approximately 39%) reached the £1,000 threshold (30 or more consultations).

IMPLICATIONS
The £71.69 million in missed payments represents revenue that pharmacies expected but did not receive, exacerbating cash flow problems. NHSBSA has been criticised for payment delays. Chain pharmacies including Boots, Superdrug, Well Pharmacy, and Day Lewis have questioned the ROI of Pharmacy First; some may reduce or cease delivery above caps. The gap between consultation cost (£22-28) and reimbursement (£17) means that even pharmacies meeting thresholds may lose money on each consultation.

INTERIM PAYMENT
The £500 interim payment introduced in June 2025 was aimed at supporting pharmacies that had struggled to meet the 30-consultation threshold. It reached 1,565 pharmacies. The payment was separate from the £1,000 threshold payment; pharmacies could receive one or the other depending on their consultation volume. Health journalists have tracked whether the interim payment adequately addresses the funding gap for low-volume pharmacies.
