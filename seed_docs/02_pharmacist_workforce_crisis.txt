PHARMACIST WORKFORCE AND FINANCIAL CRISIS IN UK COMMUNITY PHARMACY
Sources: PDA Survey 2025, BBC News, Pharmacy Magazine

STAFFING CRISIS
According to a Pharmacists' Defence Association (PDA) survey:
- 75% of pharmacists report their pharmacies lack adequate staffing to safely deliver Pharmacy First and other NHS services
- 93% of respondents said staffing levels had NOT been uplifted to support the Pharmacy First service despite generating nearly 5 million consultations in its first year
- Pharmacists are expected to deliver complex clinical pathway consultations on top of existing dispensing workloads
- The PDA warns this creates patient safety risks

FINANCIAL STRAIN ON PHARMACY OWNERS
- 60% of pharmacy owners in Wales have remortgaged homes or used personal savings in the last year to keep their businesses operating
- Pharmacies are dispensing essential medicines at a loss - for example paying £3.75 for aspirin but receiving only £3.05 in NHS reimbursement
- Payment delays from NHSBSA have compounded cash flow problems
- Health minister Zubir Ahmed faced intense parliamentary questioning over NHSBSA's failure to pay pharmacies for Pharmacy First consultations
- Payments were reportedly "withheld on a technicality" with many pharmacies unaware money was missing

GP REFERRAL CHALLENGES
- Some GPs are reportedly reluctant to refer patients to Pharmacy First or unclear about clinical pathways
- This creates frustration on both sides - pharmacists invested in delivering the service but cannot generate sufficient referral volume
- Over 1,500 pharmacies qualified for a lower Pharmacy First payment threshold introduced in June 2025
- Pharmacies delivering 20-29 consultations receive £500 interim fees
- Pharmacies delivering 30+ consultations receive £1,000 monthly payments

IMPACT OF CAPS ON WORKFORCE MORALE
- The payment cap system means pharmacists who grow their clinical service are punished financially
- Staff who have undergone additional clinical training feel their expertise is devalued
- Junior pharmacists qualifying with independent prescribing skills from summer 2026 face a sector that cannot financially sustain their clinical ambitions
- Union representatives warn of an "exodus" from community pharmacy to hospital or GP practice roles
