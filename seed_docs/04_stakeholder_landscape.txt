PHARMACY FIRST STAKEHOLDER LANDSCAPE AND TENSIONS
Analysis Document - March 2026

KEY STAKEHOLDER GROUPS AND THEIR POSITIONS

1. PHARMACY OWNERS (INDEPENDENTS)
- Facing existential financial pressure with rising costs and capped revenue
- Many are sole proprietors who have personally invested savings into their businesses
- Angry that the cap system punishes pharmacies that successfully grow their clinical services
- Fear that caps will force them to turn away patients or reduce opening hours
- Some considering leaving the sector entirely

2. PHARMACY OWNERS (MULTIPLES - Boots, Day Lewis, Well Pharmacy, Superdrug)
- Large chains can absorb short-term losses better than independents
- But corporate strategy is increasingly questioning the ROI of Pharmacy First
- Boots has been closing stores and consolidating - caps add further revenue uncertainty
- Day Lewis operates as a family-owned business and is more vulnerable than Boots
- Well Pharmacy is the largest independent chain with ~740 stores
- Strategic concern: caps disincentivise investment in clinical infrastructure

3. FRONTLINE PHARMACISTS
- Trained and motivated to deliver clinical services but frustrated by workload
- Payment caps feel like a devaluation of their clinical skills
- 75% report inadequate staffing for safe Pharmacy First delivery
- Growing interest in moving to GP practice or hospital roles where clinical work is better supported
- Newly qualified pharmacists (from 2026) have independent prescribing qualifications but face a sector that may not sustain clinical careers

4. PHARMACY TECHNICIANS
- Taking on expanded dispensing roles to free pharmacists for clinical work
- Concerned about accountability when pharmacists are stretched between consultations and checking
- Would benefit from clearer career progression tied to clinical service expansion

5. GENERAL PRACTITIONERS (GPs)
- Mixed views on Pharmacy First
- Some welcome reduced minor illness workload
- Others reluctant to refer, viewing pharmacists as less qualified for clinical decision-making
- Concerns about patient safety when pharmacists are already under workload pressure
- Some frustrated by poor IT integration between pharmacy and GP systems

6. PATIENTS
- Generally positive about Pharmacy First when they can access it
- Confused about which conditions qualify and where to go
- Unaware of the cap system and its implications for access
- Risk of being turned away from pharmacies that have hit their cap
- Rural patients especially dependent on pharmacy access as GP availability declines

7. NHS ENGLAND / DHSC
- Defending caps as necessary budget management
- Pharmacy First seen as a success story in reducing GP workload
- 5 million consultations in year one cited as evidence of impact
- Political pressure to demonstrate value for money from the £215m investment
- Negotiating the 2026/27 framework with Community Pharmacy England

8. COMMUNITY PHARMACY ENGLAND (CPE)
- Representing pharmacy owners in negotiations
- Calling for sustainable funding and removal of caps
- Warning that the prescribing service cannot work "without new money"
- Advocating for pharmacy's expanded clinical role
- Balancing between celebrating the funding increase and criticising the cap constraints

9. PHARMACISTS' DEFENCE ASSOCIATION (PDA - Union)
- Focused on workforce conditions, patient safety, and pharmacist wellbeing
- Publishing surveys highlighting understaffing and safety risks
- Warning of a potential "exodus" from community pharmacy
- Calling for mandatory staffing levels linked to service delivery requirements

10. HEALTH JOURNALISTS AND MEDIA
- Interested in "NHS rationing" and patient access angles
- "Pharmacists working for free" is a compelling headline
- Potential for stories about patients turned away from pharmacies
- Welsh pharmacy crisis (remortgaging homes) already generating coverage
- BBC, Pharmaceutical Journal, Chemist+Druggist, Pharmacy Magazine all covering the sector
