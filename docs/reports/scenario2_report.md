# Predictive Analysis Report: Impact of Removing Pharmacy First Payment Caps (Flat CPCF Funding) on UK Pharmacy Sector Over 6 Months

> **Scenario 2 (Counterfactual):** NHS England removes all Pharmacy First payment caps -- pharmacies are paid the full £17 fee per consultation with no monthly limit. However, total CPCF funding stays flat at 2025/26 levels (£3.073 billion baseline). This simulation explores whether uncapped payments improve sector outcomes compared to the capped scenario.

---

## Financial Relief and Budget Pressure

The removal of payment caps provides immediate financial relief for high-performing pharmacies, particularly larger chains and urban practices with high consultation volumes. However, the flat CPCF envelope creates a fundamental tension: uncapped per-consultation payments within a fixed total budget risk exhausting the funding pool faster than planned.

> "Removing caps was the right call -- our pharmacy can now plan staffing around actual demand rather than rationing consultations. But I worry what happens in month 8 when the money runs out." -- Independent pharmacy owner in simulation

**Short-term Financial Improvement**

In the first 3 months, pharmacies report improved cash flow as they can bill for all Pharmacy First consultations without artificial limits. The previous Frontier Economics finding that 47% of pharmacy branches were unprofitable at EBITDA level shows early signs of improvement, with pharmacies able to recoup more of their consultation costs at the £17 rate.

**Budget Exhaustion Risk**

NHS England officials express growing concern about budget sustainability. Without caps acting as a natural brake on spending, aggregate Pharmacy First expenditure accelerates. Financial modelling within the simulation suggests the fixed CPCF envelope could be consumed 15-20% faster than under the capped regime, potentially requiring mid-year emergency budget negotiations or retrospective payment adjustments.

> "The uncapped model is more clinically honest, but it creates a fiscal cliff. We've shifted the problem from individual pharmacy rationing to system-level budget pressure." -- NHS England policy analyst

**Differential Impact Across Pharmacy Types**

Larger chain pharmacies (Boots, Well, Day Lewis) benefit disproportionately from uncapped payments due to higher consultation volumes. Independent pharmacies see modest improvement but remain financially constrained by underlying CPCF funding levels. The structural divide between chains and independents persists, though the mechanism shifts from cap-induced rationing to volume-driven inequality.

## Workforce Retention and Service Delivery

**Improved Morale, Lingering Concerns**

The removal of caps produces a measurable improvement in pharmacist morale. The PDA's January 2025 survey finding that 75% of pharmacists felt pharmacies were inadequately staffed shows early signs of stabilisation as some pharmacists defer planned departures from community pharmacy. The perverse incentive to refuse patients at the cap threshold is eliminated.

> "I no longer have to choose between turning patients away and working for free. That's a meaningful change." -- Community pharmacist

However, the underlying staffing crisis persists. The 93-94% of pharmacists reporting no increase in staffing despite Pharmacy First demands remains unchanged, as the flat CPCF budget does not fund additional workforce investment. The removal of caps addresses a symptom (consultation rationing) without curing the disease (systemic underfunding of pharmacy workforce).

**Enhanced Patient Access**

Patient access improves notably in the short term. Pharmacies are more willing to take on Pharmacy First consultations for sore throat, UTIs, earache, sinusitis, and impetigo without financial penalty. GP practices report early signs of reduced pressure from minor illness presentations being successfully managed in community pharmacy.

> "For the first time since Pharmacy First launched, I can see it working as intended. Patients are getting seen, pharmacists are getting paid." -- GP practice partner

However, the simulation predicts that this improvement is fragile. If budget pressure leads to mid-year funding adjustments or payment delays, the access gains are at risk of rapid reversal.

## Stakeholder Reactions and Political Dynamics

**Cautious Optimism from Pharmacy Sector**

Community Pharmacy England (CPE) welcomes the removal of caps but maintains pressure for overall CPCF uplift, arguing that flat funding within an uncapped system merely delays rather than resolves the sustainability crisis. The PDA adopts a watchful stance, monitoring workforce outcomes and warning against complacency.

> "Removing caps is necessary but not sufficient. Without a real-terms funding increase, we're just rearranging deck chairs on a sinking ship." -- CPE negotiating team statement

**NHS England's Fiscal Tightrope**

NHS England finds itself defending an approach that is more popular with providers but creates unpredictable cost exposure. The simulation shows internal tension between policy teams who support the clinical model and finance teams alarmed by spending trajectories that exceed budgeted forecasts.

**Political Recalibration**

Unlike the capped scenario where pharmacy closures and patient denial stories dominated media, the uncapped scenario produces a more nuanced political environment. MPs report fewer constituent complaints about pharmacy access, but opposition scrutiny shifts to budget management and the sustainability of the CPCF settlement.

> "The government has traded one political problem for another. Instead of answering for pharmacy closures, they'll be answering for budget overruns." -- Health select committee member

## Six-Month Outlook

The simulation predicts that by month 6, the uncapped scenario produces **better short-term outcomes** than the capped alternative across three key metrics:

1. **Financial Viability:** Pharmacy profitability improves modestly (estimated 5-8% improvement in average EBITDA margins), though the structural deficit remains.
2. **Workforce Retention:** Community pharmacy workforce attrition slows but does not reverse. The exodus to hospital and GP practice roles continues at a reduced rate.
3. **Patient Access:** Pharmacy First consultation volumes increase by an estimated 12-18% compared to the capped scenario, with fewer patient refusals.

However, the flat CPCF budget creates a **medium-term fiscal cliff**. Without additional funding, NHS England faces three unappealing options by Q3/Q4:

- **Retrospective payment reductions** that would destroy sector trust
- **Reintroduction of modified caps** that would trigger political backlash
- **Emergency budget supplementation** that conflicts with Treasury fiscal constraints

The simulation concludes that removing caps is a necessary but insufficient reform. The fundamental challenge -- delivering expanded clinical services through community pharmacy within a funding envelope that has not kept pace with demand or inflation -- remains unresolved regardless of whether individual consultation payments are capped or uncapped.
